# Day 04 Concepts — Measurement, Probability, and Amplitudes

## Why this day matters

Connect quantum states to the classical data obtained from measurements.

## 1. Born rule

Measurement probabilities are computed from squared amplitude magnitudes.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## 2. Basis choice

A state that looks deterministic in one basis can look probabilistic in another. Basis choice is therefore part of the experiment.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## 3. Collapse

After a projective measurement, the post-measurement state is consistent with the observed outcome for that measurement basis.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## 4. Shots

Because quantum outcomes are probabilistic, circuits are often executed repeatedly. Each repetition is a shot.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## 5. Sampling error

Finite-shot estimates fluctuate around true probabilities. More shots generally reduce sampling uncertainty, though they do not fix hardware noise or model error.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## Connect the ideas

Do not treat these concepts as isolated vocabulary. Ask how they change the way you predict a circuit's output, evaluate a hardware result, or judge an application claim.

## What to be able to say out loud

Explain today's topic in three levels:

1. **30 seconds:** one-sentence definition and why it matters.
2. **2 minutes:** include one mathematical or circuit-level example.
3. **5 minutes:** include assumptions, limitations, and one connection to another quantum concept.

## Common learning trap

The most common mistake is memorizing definitions without predicting behavior. Whenever possible, write down what you expect to happen **before** calculating, simulating, or measuring.
