# Day 02 Concepts — Bits vs Qubits

## Why this day matters

Understand how classical bits and qubits represent information differently.

## 1. Classical bit

A classical bit is observed as either 0 or 1. Classical state vectors can still be used mathematically, but the physical bit itself is not prepared in arbitrary coherent superpositions.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## 2. Qubit state

A pure qubit can be written as |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, where α and β are complex amplitudes and |α|² + |β|² = 1.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## 3. Global vs relative phase

A global phase does not change observable probabilities, while relative phase can affect interference and therefore future measurement statistics.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## 4. Bloch sphere

The Bloch sphere is a geometric picture for pure single-qubit states. North and south poles represent |0⟩ and |1⟩; other surface points represent superpositions.

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## 5. Measurement

A standard computational-basis measurement returns 0 with probability |α|² and 1 with probability |β|².

**Think about it:** What assumption does this idea rely on, and how would you recognize it in a circuit or system?

## Connect the ideas

Do not treat these concepts as isolated vocabulary. Ask how they change the way you predict a circuit's output, evaluate a hardware result, or judge an application claim.

## What to be able to say out loud

Explain today's topic in three levels:

1. **30 seconds:** one-sentence definition and why it matters.
2. **2 minutes:** include one mathematical or circuit-level example.
3. **5 minutes:** include assumptions, limitations, and one connection to another quantum concept.

## Common learning trap

The most common mistake is memorizing definitions without predicting behavior. Whenever possible, write down what you expect to happen **before** calculating, simulating, or measuring.
